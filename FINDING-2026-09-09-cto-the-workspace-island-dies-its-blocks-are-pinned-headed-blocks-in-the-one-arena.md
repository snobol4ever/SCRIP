# FINDING 2026-09-09 (cto) — the workspace island dies: its blocks are PINNED headed blocks in the one arena, immortal, unmoved, scanned as roots

Row: `snobol4-icon-shared-engine-zeta-workspace-island-dies-headed-gc-blocks-no-malloc` (CEO-450 Lon: *"Get rid of the workspace island. Gone girl!"* · CEO-451: *"Replace it with proper static or heap or ZETA storage."* · CEO-454 the approved plan · CEO-474 the split from the ζ row after the coo's audit).

## What the island was, measured before cutting

A 1 GB `rt_slab_region` beside the GC arena, bump-allocated from both ends (values up, strings down), whose blocks carried the same `rt_hblk_t` header as arena blocks. The collector treated it as three things at once: a ROOT SET (the whole `[base, top)` range scanned conservatively on every non-PZ collection), an IMMORTAL store (nothing in it was ever freed), and a PINNED store (nothing in it ever moved, while the arena proper is compacted by a forwarding pass). `rt_ws_alloc` had 349 mentions, `rt_ws_strdup` 228, `rt_ws_strdup_c` 127 -- the runtime's value-string and table allocator wearing an island name -- and a hand-written asm twin bumped the slab pointers directly.

## The cure

The island's semantics are a KIND of block, not a place: `hb_pinned(type)` names the four island types (`HB_WS`, `HB_WSS`, `HB_DINST`, `HB_ARR`). The collector marks every pinned block unconditionally before the worklist runs and scans it conservatively like an activation frame (the root property); the forwarding pass forwards a pinned block to itself and advances the destination past it, so the slide leaves it in place with a FILL hole before it that becomes an allocation window (the pinned property); nothing frees it (the immortal property). `rt_pinned_alloc_core` carves the block in the arena through the one carve and collects only when the arena is exhausted, so no new collection point enters code that held raw pointers across an island allocation. `gc_pinned_exact` recognizes a DT_A/DT_DATA pointer by the arena index instead of the island index. The slab, `rt_wsi_init`, the banner, the index and the asm bump are deleted; the asm entry falls through to the C allocator.

Names: `rt_ws_alloc/_tag/_realloc/_strdup` → `rt_pinned_alloc/_tag/_realloc/_strdup` at every caller; `rt_ws_alloc_c/_strdup_c`, which always allocated in the heap proper, → `rt_heap_alloc_c/_strdup_c`.

## The four lifetime sites, by name (CEO-451)

- `driver_data.c` DATA prototypes and field tables: load-fixed, PROCESS LIFETIME -- pinned blocks, the storage class they had.
- `prolog_atom.c` atom names and hash table, `pl_cell.h` overflow cells: run-time growth, HEAP -- pinned blocks, immortal as before.
- `driver_call.c` saved-name/saved-value tables: stored into the frame record (`fr->saved_names`) and restored when the callee returns, so their lifetime is the CALLEE'S ACTIVATION and their home is the callee's frame; they are still pinned blocks here (one per call, never freed, exactly as on the island). Re-homing them is a frame-record layout change and is the residue this FINDING names, not a malloc.
